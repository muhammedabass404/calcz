Official Circumference Tape Protocol (AR 600-9 Appendix B)

Department of the Army standardized instructions for administering DA Form 5500 and 5501 assessments.

1

Equipment Specifications

Use a non-stretchable measuring tape made of fiberglass or reinforced cloth (1/4 to 3/8 inch wide). Metal builder tapes are strictly prohibited. Calibrate the tape against a standardized ruler quarterly.

2

Two-Person Test Requirement

Taping requires two trained individuals: a primary measurer and a designated recorder. Measurers must be of the same gender as the soldier being taped, or a same-gender observer must be present.

3

Sequential 3-Measurement Rule

Take three sequential measurements at each site in succession rather than three consecutive readings at a single site. Average the three readings and round to the nearest 0.5 inch (or 0.1 cm).

4

Tension & Breathing Control

The tape must be applied with uniform tension without indenting the skin or compressing underlying subcutaneous fat. Abdomen measurements must be recorded at the end of a normal, relaxed exhalation.

Precise Anatomical Landmarks

Neck (Both Genders): Place the tape immediately below the thyroid cartilage (larynx / Adam's apple). The soldier looks straight ahead with chin parallel to the floor. The tape slopes slightly downward in the front.
Abdomen (Male): Align the tape horizontally directly across the midpoint of the navel (belly button). Arms hang naturally at the sides. Measurements must not be taken over heavy seams or belts.
Waist (Female): Place the tape horizontally at the point of minimal abdominal circumference, typically located midway between the lowest rib and the iliac crest (iliac margin).
Hips (Female): View from the side to identify the maximal protrusion of the gluteal muscles (widest point). Keep the tape strictly horizontal around the circumference of the pelvis.

Complete Guide to Army Regulation 600-9 and the Military Body Fat Tape Test

Published by: CalcWorld Editorial Health Team • Regulatory Reference: AR 600-9 & DoD Instruction 1308.3 • Last Updated: 2026 Operational Standards
Official Defense Regulatory Notice: This calculator provides mathematical estimations matching Department of the Army Regulation 600-9 equations. It does not replace an official commander-directed diagnostic or record weigh-in administered by authorized unit physical fitness personnel. Official results must be documented on DA Form 5500 (Male) or DA Form 5501 (Female).

1. The Operational Framework of the Army Body Composition Program (ABCP)

The United States Army established the Army Body Composition Program (ABCP) under Army Regulation 600-9 to maintain an operationally ready, lethal force. The regulation dictates that every soldier must present a trim, athletic military appearance and possess the physical endurance required for worldwide tactical deployment.

While general civilians often rely on simple Body Mass Index (BMI) charts to gauge health, the armed services recognized decades ago that BMI unfairly penalizes heavily muscled service members. High skeletal muscle mass increases scale weight without adding adipose tissue. To solve this dilemma, the Department of Defense adopted circumference-based equations established through extensive research by the Naval Health Research Center.

Under AR 600-9, soldiers are screened semiannually against the Weight-for-Height screening table (Table B-1). If a soldier's weight falls at or below the screening threshold for their height and age group, they meet the standard without undergoing tape measurement. If a soldier exceeds the screening weight, or if a commander observes that the soldier presents an untrim appearance regardless of scale weight, a formal circumference tape test becomes mandatory.

2. Mathematical Tape Test Formulas (DA Form 5500 and 5501)

The equations powering DA Form 5500 (Body Fat Content Worksheet - Male) and DA Form 5501 (Body Fat Content Worksheet - Female) derive directly from Department of Defense Instruction 1308.3. These logarithmic relationships correlate body circumference ratios with underwater weighing (hydrostatic densitometry) data.

DA Form 5500 (Male Equation) % Body Fat = 86.010 × log10(Abdomen − Neck) − 70.041 × log10(Height) + 36.76 All inputs entered in inches. Abdomen measured at navel.
DA Form 5501 (Female Equation) % Body Fat = 163.205 × log10(Waist + Hip − Neck) − 97.684 × log10(Height) − 78.387 All inputs entered in inches. Waist at narrowest point; hips at widest point.

A critical regulatory detail in AR 600-9 involves rounding. Individual tape circumference readings are rounded to the nearest half inch (0.5 in). When calculating the final body fat percentage, the Army truncates fractions of a percent down to the next lower whole integer. For example, a calculated value of 22.9% is officially recorded on the worksheet as 22%, granting the soldier the benefit of the mathematical round-down.

3. Maximum Allowable Body Fat Limits by Age Bracket

Because physiological body composition naturally changes as individuals mature, AR 600-9 establishes four distinct age brackets. Soldiers transition into their new bracket on their birthday, with the allowable body fat standard expanding incrementally to account for natural physiological shifts.

Age Bracket Male Maximum Body Fat Female Maximum Body Fat Regulatory Rationale
17 to 20 Years 20% 30% Entry-level recruits; highest metabolic baseline expectation.
21 to 27 Years 22% 32% Junior leaders and noncommissioned officers in prime operational condition.
28 to 39 Years 24% 34% Mid-career soldiers with accumulated tactical physical conditioning.
40 Years and Older 26% 36% Senior commanders, staff officers, and master sergeants.

4. Army Directive 2023-08 Updates: Single-Site Tape and ACFT 540 Exemption

In March 2023, the Secretary of the Army issued Army Directive 2023-08 (Army Body Composition Program Policy Updates), representing one of the most substantial modernizations of military fitness policy in decades. Following a comprehensive multi-year study conducted by the U.S. Army Research Institute of Environmental Medicine (USARIEM), the service introduced two critical provisions:

A. The High-Scoring ACFT Exemption (540 Points / 80 Minimum in Each Event)

Under Directive 2023-08, any soldier who scores 540 points or higher on the diagnostic or record Army Combat Fitness Test (ACFT), with at least 80 points scored in each of the six events, is entirely exempt from height and weight tape testing. The Army determined that soldiers demonstrating this level of comprehensive muscular strength, power, and cardiovascular endurance possess acceptable combat readiness regardless of scale weight or tape circumference.

B. The Single-Site Circumference Protocol and Fallback Rule

Directive 2023-08 also authorized an expedited single-site tape test (navel circumference for both genders) as an optional preliminary screening. Crucially, the directive guarantees soldiers a vital protection: if a soldier fails the single-site tape assessment, the command must tape the soldier using the traditional multi-site circumference formula (DA Form 5500/5501) before taking any adverse action or enrolling the soldier into the ABCP.

5. Circumference Measurement Guidelines (DA Form 5500 / 5501 Protocol)

To ensure objective standardization across the entire force, AR 600-9 Appendix B mandates exact measurement techniques. Failure by test administrators to adhere to these rules invalidates the test:

  • Uniform State: Measurements must be taken with the soldier dressed in the authorized Army Physical Fitness Uniform (APFU), consisting of running shorts and short-sleeve shirt without shoes, jackets, or bulky undergarments.
  • Sequential Rotation: The measuring team must take one measurement at each required site, then repeat the entire sequence two more times. Taking three consecutive measurements at a single site before moving to the next is a regulatory error that distorts results.
  • Tape Tension: The tape must rest flat against the skin without twisting. It must be pulled taut enough to eliminate slack, but not tight enough to indent the skin or compress subcutaneous fat tissue.
  • Breathing Cycle: Abdomen and waist readings must be recorded at the natural end of a normal, relaxed exhalation. Soldiers are instructed not to hold their breath or suck in their stomach.

6. Worked Numerical Examples

Scenario A: Sergeant Miller (Male, Age 25)

Height: 71 inches | Weight: 198 lbs | Age Bracket: 21–27 (Max Allowable: 22%)

Screening Table B-1 Check: Max screening weight for 71 inches at age 25 is 190 lbs. Weight of 198 lbs exceeds screening limit by 8 lbs. Tape test required.

Tape Measurements: Neck = 16.5 inches | Abdomen = 34.5 inches.

Circumference Difference = 34.5 − 16.5 = 18.0
% BF = 86.010 × log10(18.0) − 70.041 × log10(71) + 36.76
% BF = 86.010 × (1.2553) − 70.041 × (1.8513) + 36.76
% BF = 107.97 − 129.67 + 36.76 = 15.06%

Result: 15% (Truncated). Sergeant Miller passes the standard with a 7% safety margin below his 22% limit.

Scenario B: Specialist Davis (Female, Age 23)

Height: 65 inches | Weight: 162 lbs | Age Bracket: 21–27 (Max Allowable: 32%)

Screening Table B-1 Check: Max screening weight for 65 inches at age 23 is 152 lbs. Weight of 162 lbs exceeds limit by 10 lbs. Tape test required.

Tape Measurements: Neck = 14.0 in | Waist = 29.5 in | Hips = 39.5 in.

Total Circumference = 29.5 + 39.5 − 14.0 = 55.0
% BF = 163.205 × log10(55.0) − 97.684 × log10(65) − 78.387
% BF = 163.205 × (1.7404) − 97.684 × (1.8129) − 78.387
% BF = 284.04 − 177.09 − 78.387 = 28.56%

Result: 28% (Truncated). Specialist Davis passes the standard with a 4% safety margin below her 32% limit.

7. Consequences of ABCP Enrollment (DA Form 268 Flag)

Failing the tape test carries severe career ramifications under military personnel law. Once a failure is certified, the commander has three working days to initiate a Suspension of Favorable Personnel Actions (Flag) using DA Form 268. The soldier must be officially enrolled in the ABCP within 30 days.

Mandatory Personnel Restrictions While Flagged:

  • Denial of promotion consideration, promotion pin-on, or advancement onto centralized selection lists.
  • Prohibition from reenlisting or executing voluntary active duty extensions.
  • Cancellation of orders to professional military education institutions (BLC, ALC, SLC, Captains Career Course).
  • Ineligibility for transfer, PCS reassignments (subject to HRC exceptions), or command selection.
  • Ineligibility to receive military awards or decorations for meritorious service.

Once enrolled, soldiers must meet with a registered dietitian or certified nutrition provider within 30 days and undergo monthly weigh-ins and tape assessments. Unsatisfactory progress—defined as failing to exhibit a loss of 3 to 8 pounds or 1% body fat per month for two consecutive months—triggers mandatory initiation of separation proceedings under AR 635-200 (Active Duty Enlisted Administrative Separations) or AR 600-8-24 for commissioned officers.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Army calculate body fat percentage? ▾

The Army uses the Department of Defense circumference equations. For male soldiers, it correlates the difference between abdominal and neck circumferences with height. For female soldiers, it correlates the sum of waist and hip circumferences minus neck circumference with height. Measurements are taken with a non-stretchable tape and averaged over three sequential cycles.

Can an ACFT score truly exempt me from the body fat tape test? ▾

Yes. Under Army Directive 2023-08, any active duty, Reserve, or National Guard soldier who scores 540 or higher on a record or diagnostic ACFT, with at least 80 points in all six events, is officially exempt from body fat tape testing, even if they exceed Table B-1 screening weights.

What is the difference between screening weight and maximum body fat? ▾

Screening weight is the scale weight limit published in Table B-1 of AR 600-9. Passing the screening weight means the soldier does not need to be taped. If a soldier is over their screening weight, they must take the tape test. Passing the tape test means the soldier is compliant with Army regulations regardless of scale weight.

How are fractions of inches and percentages rounded? ▾

Circumferences are rounded to the nearest half-inch (0.5 in). For final body fat percentages, AR 600-9 dictates that mathematical fractions are truncated down to the nearest whole integer. For example, 22.8% truncates to 22%.

Can pregnant soldiers be taped or enrolled in ABCP? ▾

No. Pregnant soldiers are exempt from body composition standards for the duration of pregnancy plus 365 days after the conclusion of pregnancy, as codified under Army Directive 2022-06 (Parenthood, Pregnancy, and Postpartum).

What should I do if I am taped incorrectly? ▾

Soldiers have the right to request a confirmatory tape test administered by a different trained team. Additionally, commanders may refer the soldier for secondary medical evaluation or dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) body composition analysis if anatomical abnormalities exist.
