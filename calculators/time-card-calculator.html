Payroll Timekeeping: FLSA Standards, Rounding Rules, and Overtime Auditing

Accurate employee time tracking is a statutory requirement under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) enforced by the United States Department of Labor (DOL). Whether administering hourly wage payroll for a small enterprise or managing multi-shift workforce scheduling, converting sexagesimal clock minutes (base-60) into decimal hours (base-10), accounting for mandatory meal breaks, and enforcing federal and state overtime thresholds is critical for regulatory compliance and avoiding back-wage litigation.

The 7-Minute Rounding Rule (Code of Federal Regulations § 785.48(b))

Under federal law, employers may round employee clock-in and clock-out timestamps to the nearest 15-minute interval (one-quarter of an hour), provided the rounding rule is applied neutrally and does not systematically withhold earned wages from workers over time:

Actual Clock Punch Minute Rounding Threshold Recorded Payroll Minute Recorded Decimal Value
:53 to :07 past the hour Nearest hour :00 0.00 hours
:08 to :22 past the hour First quarter hour :15 0.25 hours
:23 to :37 past the hour Half hour :30 0.50 hours
:38 to :52 past the hour Third quarter hour :45 0.75 hours

Converting Clock Minutes to Decimal Payroll Hours

Because payroll software computes wages by multiplying hours by hourly wage rates, sexagesimal minutes must be converted to base-10 decimals:

\text{Total Decimal Hours} = \text{Whole Hours} + \left( \frac{\text{Minutes}}{60} ight)

For example, an employee working 8 hours and 27 minutes worked 8 + (27 / 60) = 8.45 decimal hours. Multiplying 8.27 by an hourly wage results in a significant payroll calculation error.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example: Weekly Payroll with Overtime

An employee earning a base rate of $22.50 per hour records the following daily shift hours across a 5-day workweek, taking an unpaid 45-minute meal break each day:

Step 1: Compute Daily Gross and Net Shift Durations
- Monday: 8:00 AM to 5:30 PM = 9h 30m gross - 45m break = 8h 45m (8.75 hours).
- Tuesday: 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM = 10h 00m gross - 45m break = 9h 15m (9.25 hours).
- Wednesday: 8:00 AM to 5:15 PM = 9h 15m gross - 45m break = 8h 30m (8.50 hours).
- Thursday: 8:00 AM to 6:30 PM = 10h 30m gross - 45m break = 9h 45m (9.75 hours).
- Friday: 8:00 AM to 4:45 PM = 8h 45m gross - 45m break = 8h 00m (8.00 hours).

Step 2: Sum Total Weekly Work Hours
Total Hours = 8.75 + 9.25 + 8.50 + 9.75 + 8.00 = 44.25 total hours.

Step 3: Separate Regular Hours from FLSA Overtime
- Regular Hours (up to 40.0) = 40.00 hours.
- Overtime Hours (hours in excess of 40.0) = 44.25 - 40.0 = 4.25 hours.

Step 4: Compute Gross Earnings
- Regular Wages: 40.00 × $22.50 = $900.00.
- Overtime Rate (1.5x time-and-a-half): $22.50 × 1.5 = $33.75 per hour.
- Overtime Wages: 4.25 × $33.75 = $143.44.
- Total Gross Pay = $900.00 + $143.44 = $1,043.44.

FLSA Meal Breaks vs. Rest Breaks Regulations

  • Rest Breaks (5 to 20 minutes): Federal law does not mandate rest breaks, but if an employer provides short breaks, DOL regulations mandate that they MUST be counted as compensable paid hours worked.
  • Bona Fide Meal Periods (30+ minutes): Meal periods are non-compensable unpaid time, provided the employee is completely relieved from all work duties. If an employee answers business phone calls or monitors equipment while eating at their desk, the meal period must be paid.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is California's daily overtime rule?

Unlike federal law, which only mandates overtime after 40 hours in a workweek, California law requires overtime (1.5x) for any hours worked beyond 8 hours in a single workday, and double-time (2.0x) for any hours worked beyond 12 hours in a single day.

Can salaried employees qualify for overtime pay?

Yes. To be exempt from overtime under the FLSA, an employee must meet both the salary basis test (earning above the statutory weekly threshold) AND the job duties test (executive, administrative, or professional duties). Salaried workers in non-exempt roles are entitled to overtime.

How do overnight shifts crossing midnight calculate?

For shifts crossing midnight (e.g., 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM), time card engines add 24 hours to the end timestamp before subtracting the start timestamp, ensuring elapsed duration calculates accurately without negative numbers.
