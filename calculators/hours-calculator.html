Payroll & Wage Compliance Guide • Updated 2026

Comprehensive Guide to Hours Calculation, FLSA Overtime Rules, & Meal Deductions

Authored by payroll compliance experts. Referenced from the US Department of Labor (DOL) Fair Labor Standards Act guidelines.

1. Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) Workweek Standards

Calculating employee hours accurately is essential for legal compliance, payroll processing, and maintaining transparent labor practices. In the United States, the primary federal law governing employee compensation is the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), enforced by the Wage and Hour Division of the US Department of Labor (DOL).

Under 29 U.S.C. § 207, covered non-exempt employees must receive overtime pay for all hours worked beyond 40 hours in a single workweek. A workweek is defined as a fixed and regularly recurring period of 168 hours—seven consecutive 24-hour periods. It does not need to coincide with the calendar week and can begin on any day and hour established by the employer.

2. Overtime Rate Mathematics: Time-and-a-Half Explained

Federal law mandates that overtime pay must be calculated at a rate not less than one and one-half times (1.5x) the employee's regular rate of pay. The regular rate is calculated by dividing the total remuneration for employment in any workweek by the total number of hours actually worked in that workweek.

Formula 1: Regular Pay Calculation
Regular Pay = Regular Hours (up to 40) × Regular Hourly Rate
Formula 2: Overtime Pay Calculation
Overtime Pay = Overtime Hours (above 40) × (Regular Hourly Rate × 1.5)
Formula 3: Total Gross Earnings
Total Gross Pay = Regular Pay + Overtime Pay

While federal law requires overtime only after 40 weekly hours, specific state jurisdictions enforce daily overtime rules. For example, California Labor Code § 510 requires overtime pay (1.5x) for any work exceeding 8 hours in a single workday or for the first 8 hours worked on the seventh consecutive day of work in a workweek, and double time (2.0x) for hours exceeding 12 hours in a single workday.

3. Lunch Break Deductions & Rest Period Rules (29 CFR 785.19)

A critical aspect of timesheet accuracy is distinguishing between compensable rest breaks and non-compensable meal periods according to Code of Federal Regulations (29 CFR § 785.19):

  • Short Rest Breaks (5 to 20 minutes): Federal regulations treat short rest periods as compensable work time. These breaks must be counted as hours worked and included in payroll calculations.
  • Bona Fide Meal Periods (30 minutes or longer): Meal breaks are non-compensable unpaid time, provided the employee is completely relieved from all duty for the purpose of eating meals. If an employee is required to perform any duties (e.g., answering office phones or monitoring machinery) while eating, the meal period must be paid as regular work hours.

4. Time Rounding Compliance: The 7/8-Minute Rule

Employers often use time clock rounding to streamline payroll processing. Under 29 CFR § 785.48(b), the Department of Labor permits employers to round employee starting and stopping times to the nearest 5 minutes, 1/10th of an hour (6 minutes), or 1/4th of an hour (15 minutes).

Under the standard 15-minute rounding scheme (the 7/8-minute rule):

Minutes 1 to 7: Rounded down to the previous quarter-hour mark (00 minutes).
Minutes 8 to 14: Rounded up to the next quarter-hour mark (15 minutes).

Important Compliance Note: Time rounding is only permissible if it is neutral over a representative period of time and does not consistently result in failure to pay employees for all hours actually worked.

5. Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Scenario

To understand how total hours and gross earnings are computed in practice, consider an employee with an hourly rate of $25.00/hr working the following schedule across 6 days:

Day Start Time End Time Lunch Break Net Worked
Monday 08:00 AM 05:00 PM (9.0 hrs) 30 mins 8.50 hrs
Tuesday 08:00 AM 05:00 PM (9.0 hrs) 30 mins 8.50 hrs
Wednesday 08:00 AM 05:00 PM (9.0 hrs) 30 mins 8.50 hrs
Thursday 08:00 AM 05:00 PM (9.0 hrs) 30 mins 8.50 hrs
Friday 08:00 AM 05:00 PM (9.0 hrs) 30 mins 8.50 hrs
Saturday 09:00 AM 02:30 PM (5.5 hrs) 30 mins 5.00 hrs

Step 1: Total Worked Hours Summation: 8.5 + 8.5 + 8.5 + 8.5 + 8.5 + 5.0 = 47.50 Total Net Hours.

Step 2: Hours Categorization:

  • Regular Hours = 40.00 hours
  • Overtime Hours = 47.50 − 40.00 = 7.50 hours

Step 3: Wage Calculation:

  • Regular Wages = 40.00 hrs × $25.00 = $1,000.00
  • Overtime Rate = $25.00 × 1.5 = $37.50/hr
  • Overtime Wages = 7.50 hrs × $37.50 = $281.25
  • Total Gross Earnings = $1,000.00 + $281.25 = $1,281.25
